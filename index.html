Important assumptions and limitations
  • The donor has sufficient available gift-tax exemption and pays no gift tax on the modeled transfer.
  • The gift is completed, and the transferred assets and subsequent appreciation are excluded from the donor's estate.
  • The donor's estate would otherwise be sufficiently taxable for all modeled appreciation to face the selected marginal estate-tax rate.
  • The same constant annual return applies whether assets are retained or gifted, with no distributions or additional contributions.
  • The model ignores income taxes, carryover basis versus potential basis adjustment at death, state taxes, fees, discounts, exemption changes, and future law changes. Those factors can materially change the overall benefit.
  • This is a one-time-gift illustration, not an annual-exclusion gifting model. It does not establish gift eligibility or remaining exemption.
How the calculation works

Future value = gift × (1 + annual return)years.

Appreciation = future value − original gift.

Hypothetical estate tax avoided = appreciation × assumed estate-tax rate.

The original gift is not counted as incremental estate-tax savings in this simplified exemption-funded model.
