How this model works
  • Individual mode: the entered estate and gift belong to one New York resident. The calculation uses one projected New York exclusion.
  • Married-couple mode: the entered estate is combined. Assets, prior gifts, and today’s gift are assumed equally allocated. Remaining assets at first death pass to the survivor without modeled first-death tax. At second death, all remaining assets are in the survivor’s estate and only one New York exclusion is used.
  • No credit-shelter trust, state QTIP planning, first-death costs, or preservation of the first spouse’s exclusion is modeled. New York does not provide federal-style portability. This is one specific outright-to-survivor scenario, not a general calculation for all married couples.
  • The slider uses $15 million of federal exemption per donor as a 2026 starting assumption, less prior gifts and capped by current assets. Both spouses are assumed able to make their allocated gifts. No deceased-spouse unused exclusion or federal gift tax is modeled. There is no automatic tax-law update.
  • The gift date is your browser’s local date when this page is opened. Settlement is the selected number of calendar years later. Dates remain visible in the result. In couple mode, the horizon means second death; no first-death date is modeled.
  • Before January 1, 2032, an otherwise includible gift is added back if settlement falls on or before the third anniversary. Exact anniversary inclusion is a conservative boundary assumption requiring review. Individual mode adds the full original gift; couple mode adds only the survivor’s assumed half. Appreciation is not added back.
  • Couple mode does not calculate any first-death gift addback or first-death tax. Short-horizon couple results therefore are not total combined estate taxes across both deaths. The gift attributed to the first spouse is not added to the survivor’s estate.
  • On or after January 1, 2032, no gift addback is modeled under the currently scheduled expiration. A future extension would require a code update.
  • The New York exclusion is projected from $7.35 million in 2026 using the entered growth assumption by calendar year. Lifetime gifts are not subtracted from that New York exclusion. Applicable gifts instead enter the New York tax base through the addback rule.
  • The current ET-706 graduated tax table and exclusion-credit phaseout are used. Between 100% and 105% of the exclusion, the credit is table tax on the reduced exclusion specified by the worksheet. The ratio is rounded to four decimal places. Above 105%, the credit is zero. This is not a tax-return preparation engine.
  • All assets are assumed subject to New York estate tax. No debts, expenses, charitable or marital deductions at settlement, out-of-state real or tangible property, QTIP adjustments, other prior gift addbacks, or special exclusions are modeled.
  • Retained and gifted assets earn the same constant return. No spending, distributions, additional gifts, income taxes, basis effects, or federal taxes are modeled. Family wealth includes only the entered current estate and the gift from it, less modeled New York tax. Prior gifts are not included in displayed wealth. Outputs are nominal dollars.
  • Educational prototype. Obtain independent tax, legal, calculation, and applicable compliance review before distributing. This page is not individualized advice or a carrier illustration.
