Checks and assumptions

    Methodology and limitations
    • Individual mode uses one estate and one donor. Two-donor mode assumes two equal, independent estates at the same modeled death date, equal prior gifts and strategies, no marital transfers, no DSUE, and equal separate insurance policies. NY exclusions are applied separately to those two estates. This differs from a survivor-estate scenario.
    • 2026 anchors: federal exclusion $15M, NY exclusion $7.35M, annual gift exclusion $19,000 per donor/recipient. Subsequent smooth growth assumptions are hypothetical—not statutory rounding or guaranteed future law. Fixed tax brackets; no automated updates.
    • Federal estate tax uses cumulative gifts and the projected credit. Only cases with no gift tax payable are supported. No prior paid gift tax, anti-clawback, GST, basis, capital-gains or other income-tax modeling. Prior gift assets are excluded from displayed wealth. No preexisting NY gift addbacks.
    • NY graduated table and exclusion-credit phaseout are modeled, including four-decimal ratio rounding. Applicable taxable gifts within three years are added back for modeled deaths before January 1, 2032. Exact anniversary inclusion is a conservative boundary assumption. A positive GRAT gift in that window is unsupported.
    • For the federal state-death-tax deduction, NY tax is allocated proportionally between the federal estate and NY addback-only gifts. The deduction for the addback-only share is excluded. Specific-case allocation and deduction eligibility require professional review; qualifying tax is assumed paid and timely claimed.
    • IDIT sale assumes a valid grantor-trust structure, no valuation discounts or recognition on the initial sale, and note valued at principal at death. Grantor tax payments are omitted. Asset liquidation is permitted to fund payments without taxes or expenses. A seed gift is required; no seed-adequacy opinion. All trust asset values are netted against the note for consolidated reporting.
    • GRAT is one level end-of-year annuity, valued using a term-certain present-value factor. It must end strictly before modeled death. The target remainder gift counts as a taxable gift. No qualified actuarial opinion, rolling GRATs, or death-during-term calculation. Annuity payments cannot exceed remaining assets; final remainder passes outside the estate.
    • Insurance is a newly issued valid trust-owned policy assumed excluded from the modeled taxable estate and continuously in force to death under the entered premium schedule. No cash values, loans, lapses, claim delays or insurability calculation. Level premiums are personally funded at the start of each year; payments stop after the selected term or before modeled death. Individual coverage for one donor, two equal individual policies for two donors—not survivorship.
    • Annual exclusions for premium gifts require confirmation of qualifying present interests. Count does not establish eligibility. Other gifts reduce annual capacity only; their cash flows are not projected. The initial gift and GRAT gift receive no annual exclusion here. Excess premium contributions use lifetime exemption.
    • Settlement purchase is a discretionary scenario assumption, not a direction binding a trustee. It assumes documented fair market value, trustee authority, asset suitability, and proper implementation. It exchanges trust cash for assets already in the taxable estate; it does not retroactively exclude those assets from estate tax. Review income-tax and fiduciary consequences separately.
    • If purchase modeling is off, the insurance stays in trust as cash and all estate tax is funded by estate resources. The model does not infer actual forced sales: additional estate resources required may be cash or investments. Existing external liquid assets are not separately classified.
    • All assets earn the same constant total return. No spending, distributions, administration expenses or separate income yield. Events: initial transfers, beginning-year premiums, annual growth, year-end note/GRAT payments, death, insurance receipt, asset purchase, tax payment. Notes maturing on the death date are paid before death.
